## Scenario 4: Network Delay Analysis

1. How long does it take for all replicas to converge?
   - Convergence time scales linearly with artificial network delay for both eventual and strong modes.
   - At 0ms delay, convergence happens in < 15ms. At 500ms delay, it takes ~500ms. At 2000ms delay, it takes ~2000ms.

2. Does the client see a stale/old value during this interval?
   - Eventual Consistency: Yes. If a client writes to Replica 1 and immediately reads from Replica 2 or 3, it will see stale/empty data until replication completes.
   - Strong Consistency: No. Since client writes block until a quorum of replicas confirms the write, subsequent reads from the quorum see the updated value.

3. What is the difference between eventual and strong consistency in this case?
   - Eventual Consistency: Write latency is immediate (0ms) and independent of network delay. However, the client is exposed to stale data during the convergence window.
   - Strong Consistency: Write latency is directly bound by network delay (e.g. ~500ms for 500ms delay, ~2000ms for 2000ms delay) because it blocks waiting for quorum. However, stale reads are avoided.
